Balance the bank before settling.

The bank needs two numbers per player: money in and final cash-out. Their difference is the player's result.

Check every rebuy and cash-out before money moves. Mainpot keeps those entries with the game so the table can resolve a mismatch first.

1. Balance the bank. Total money in must equal total final stacks.
2. Calculate results. Cash-out minus money in gives each player’s net.
3. Route payments. Players who owe pay those who are owed.
See the ledger move from input to settlement.

A missing add-on creates a $10.00 mismatch.

In this $0.25/$0.50 game, Alex and Sam rebuy, Jordan adds $20, and Casey adds $10. Casey's add-on is missing, so the ledger shows $300.00 while $310.00 is in play.

The five final stacks total $310.00. The $10.00 gap is an incomplete entry, not a rounding problem.

The calculator should stop here.

Calculate each player's net result.

The basic calculation is the same for every player. A positive result means the player should receive money. A negative result means the player owes money. A zero means the player is already square.

SETTLEMENT FORMULA

player net = final cash-out − total money in

Morgan

$132.25 − $40.00 = +$92.25

Casey

$0.00 − $50.00 = −$50.00

Morgan and Jordan receive $132.75 total. Alex, Sam, and Casey owe the same $132.75. All player results therefore add up to zero.

Without Casey's missing add-on, the losses total only $122.75 and cannot fund the winners' $132.75.

Turn those balances into payments.

Settlement uses final net positions, not individual hands. Players who owe fund players who should receive until every balance reaches zero.

Special cases
A buy-in was advanced

Record the player receiving the bank chips and, only while repayment is outstanding, the player who advanced the cash. Mark it repaid if they settle during the game.

One cash-out is missing

Do not treat a blank as zero unless the player actually busted. A missing final stack keeps the table from proving that the bank balances.

The host acts as the bank

A bank-style settlement can route every payment through one person. It is easier to coordinate, but may create more transfers than direct netting.

The totals differ by cents

Use the same currency precision for purchases, cash-outs, and payments. Fix the source entry instead of hiding a rounding difference in the final list.
